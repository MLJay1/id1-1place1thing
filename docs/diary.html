Decided on Blinkist for now: Blinkist as an audio Podcast app costs its users attention in short, repeated fragments.
At it's core are short audio sessions (roughly 15 minutes),
which are supposed to give you the central ideas of a much longer book in a condensed form.
They are intentionally positioned as things to listen to while working, travelling, doing chores, etc.
As far as cognitive load goes, the user still has to listen and understand the material.
As condensed books, the podcasts must necessarily remove some of the content of the original works,
meaning not all arguments, examples, context and nuance from the original author are retained.
An additional aspect is user retention/gamification where there are daily personalized recommendations (algorithms).
Interestingly, in this way blinkist can capture previously quiet moments:
If a user would have previously spent 15 minutes on the train daydreaming, now their attention is spent on an audio podcast.
It teaches in a highly dynamic way where you can learn something pretty much
anywhere at any time due to the bite-sized nature of the podcasts and it being a phone app, instead of sitting down at a desk and reading a book.

- On the other hand, there are the farmers themselves who sell their products at these farmers' markets.
- The shrinking number of customers and visitors at these markets is disadvantageous for the farmers because they sell fewer products.
- This could lead to multiple stands closing because they earn less money from fewer customers.
- Buying food from local farmers can be more sustainable than getting it from the supermarket.

Effort moved onto the user:
An instance of this happening is with the Google Keep app. It used to be so that you could write a note and instantly set a reminder for that note from the note creation screen. Now, you need to leave the note editing screen, long-press the note you've just made, and then select the "set notification" button. They likely optimized for interface complexity with this move, as hiding the button behind the secondary long-press action makes the primary note creation screen simpler, at the expense of users who often use the reminder feature.
